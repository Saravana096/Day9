My Favorite Book

Crime and Punishment

Fyodor Dostoevsky’s Crime and Punishment follows Rodion Raskolnikov, an impoverished former student in 1860s St. Petersburg.
He murders a corrupt pawnbroker, believing himself to be an "extraordinary" man above the law, but is subsequently consumed by
severe guilt, paranoia, and a feverish descent into psychological torment

The Climax and Resolution

The Confession: Broken down by Sonia’s unwavering faith, his own unbearable guilt, and Porfiry Petrovich’s intellectual pressure,
Raskolnikov finally confesses his crime to the authorities

Siberia and Redemption: Because of his confession and the fact that he actually stole very little, he receives a mitigated sentence of
eight years of hard labor in a Siberian prison. Sonia follows him there. In the prison, Raskolnikov ultimately abandons his nihilistic
theories, embraces Christian love, and finds the possibility of spiritual renewal.
